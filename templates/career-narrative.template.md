# Career narrative (cover letter spine)

Optional. When this file has real content, every cover letter is written as a
chronological, first person career story with one throughline: where you
started, how each role built on the last, what you learned at each step, and
where that points now. It is not a resume in prose and not a list of figures.
Each letter is built from this spine and then framed toward the target role.

When this file is absent, or still holds only these placeholders, cover letters
use the standard three paragraph form instead.

Rules for whatever you write here:

- Real facts only. Every employer, date, and figure must also appear in your
  resume baseline or your evidence file (reference.md).
- Keep the most recent part consistent with your current resume timeline.
- No term from `output.forbiddenTerms` in data/profile.json and nothing listed
  under "What not to use in output" in your _profile.md.
- No em dash, en dash, smart quote or other typographic punctuation; letters
  of the language you write in are kept.

Delete the bracketed prompts below as you fill each part in.

## The arc (in order)

1. [Origin. What first pulled you toward this kind of work: a course, a first
   job, a problem you kept returning to. One or two sentences.]
2. [First role or training. What you did, what it taught you, and what it left
   you wanting to do next.]
3. [Next role. How it built on the one before: wider scope, a harder problem, a
   new setting, a team to lead. Name one concrete result.]
4. [Add one numbered item per era, oldest to newest. A change of field belongs
   here too: say what carried over.]
5. [Current role. What you own now, the result you are proudest of, and the
   decision it informed.]

## The throughline (the thesis every letter lands)

[One paragraph. The single idea that connects every step above: the kind of
problem you keep choosing and the way you go about it.]

Every letter closes looking forward: it names the target company, team, or
product, and says why the next step of this arc belongs there.
